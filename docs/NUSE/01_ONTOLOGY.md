# NUSE Ontology

## Purpose

This document defines the formal ontology of the NBA Universal Simulation Engine (NUSE).

The ontology specifies every fundamental object that exists inside the simulated NBA universe.

No formulas, algorithms or implementations are defined here.

This document answers one question only:

**What exists?**

---

# 1. Definition of the NBA Universe

Within NUSE, the NBA is represented as a hierarchy of entities interacting through causal relationships across time.

Every simulation is an evolution of this universe.

Nothing may exist outside this ontology unless explicitly added in future specifications.

---

# 2. Fundamental Principles

The ontology follows five immutable principles.

## Existence

Every object must exist as an identifiable entity.

## Hierarchy

Every entity belongs to a hierarchical level.

## Causality

Every observable result originates from previous causes.

## Time

Everything evolves over time.

No variable is independent from temporal context.

## Interaction

Entities continuously interact with one another.

No entity is completely isolated.

# 4. Core Entities

The ontology recognizes the following core entities.

ENTITY_NBA

Represents the entire league.

---

ENTITY_SEASON

Represents one NBA season.

Contains every scheduled game and all league-wide context.

---

ENTITY_CONFERENCE

Eastern Conference.

Western Conference.

---

ENTITY_DIVISION

League subdivision.

---

ENTITY_FRANCHISE

Represents the long-term organization.

Independent from seasonal roster.

Examples include ownership, market, organizational stability and long-term philosophy.

---

ENTITY_TEAM

Represents one franchise during one specific season.

Contains:

Roster

Coaching Staff

Rotation

System

Schedule

Performance

---

ENTITY_COACH

Represents the complete coaching staff.

Contains tactical philosophy, player development, rotation management and strategic behaviour.

---

ENTITY_PLAYER

Represents one basketball player.

Players are collections of attributes, capabilities and dynamic states.

Players are NOT collections of statistics.

Statistics emerge from player behaviour.

---

ENTITY_LINEUP

Represents any combination of players simultaneously on the court.

Lineups possess emergent properties not attributable to individual players alone.

Examples include:

Spacing

Switchability

Interior defense

Playmaking density

Offensive gravity

Chemistry

---

ENTITY_GAME

Represents one NBA game.

Games consist of ordered possessions.

---

ENTITY_QUARTER

Represents one game segment.

---

ENTITY_POSSESSION

Represents the smallest strategic unit of basketball.

Every offensive opportunity is modeled as one possession.

Every statistic ultimately originates from possessions.

---

ENTITY_EVENT

Represents one atomic basketball action.

Examples:

Pass

Screen

Cut

Drive

Shot

Rebound

Turnover

Foul

Steal

Block

Substitution

Timeout

Jump Ball

---

ENTITY_STATISTICAL_OUTPUT

Represents every measurable outcome generated by previous events.

Examples:

Points

Assists

Rebounds

Minutes

Usage Rate

BPM

PER

VORP

Win Shares

No statistical output exists independently.

Every statistic is the consequence of previous events.

---

ENTITY_HISTORICAL_RECORD

Represents permanent historical information produced after simulation.

Examples:

Season totals

Career totals

Awards

Records

Historical rankings

---

# 6. State

Every entity possesses a state.

A state represents the complete description of that entity at one moment in time.

States evolve continuously.

Examples:

Player fatigue

Player confidence

Team chemistry

Injury status

Rotation role

Coach strategy

---

# 7. Attributes

Entities contain attributes.

Attributes describe the current state of an entity.

Attributes are not calculations.

Examples:

Age

Height

Weight

Experience

Contract

Position

Handedness

Injury status

Coach philosophy

---

# 8. Capabilities

Capabilities describe what an entity is able to perform.

Examples:

Shooting ability

Passing ability

Rim protection

Perimeter defense

Ball handling

Court vision

Off-ball movement

Capabilities are latent properties.

Capabilities generate decisions.

---

# 9. Decisions

Entities make decisions.

Examples:

Shoot

Pass

Drive

Cut

Post Up

Screen

Help Defense

Switch

Double Team

Intentional Foul

Take Charge

Decisions generate events.

---

# 10. Events

Events are observable basketball actions.

Events occur inside possessions.

Events modify the state of the universe.

Examples:

Made shot

Missed shot

Assist

Block

Steal

Rebound

Turnover

Every event has causes.

Every event has consequences.

---

# 11. Statistics

Statistics summarize previous events.

Statistics never cause basketball.

Basketball causes statistics.

This distinction is fundamental throughout the NUSE specification.

---

# 12. Context

Every entity exists inside a context.

Player context includes:

Team

Coach

Role

Teammates

Opponents

Schedule

Travel

Rest

Arena

Crowd

Injuries

League environment

No prediction may ignore context.

---

# 13. Environment

The NBA itself possesses global properties.

Examples:

League Pace

League Efficiency

League Rules

Officiating Trends

Three Point Era

Salary Cap

Collective Bargaining Agreement

Expansion

Rule Changes

Environment affects every entity simultaneously.

---

# 14. Emergence

Complex behaviour emerges from interactions.

Examples:

Championship teams.

Elite defenses.

Historic offenses.

Player development.

Breakout seasons.

No emergent behaviour shall be hardcoded.

Emergence is produced by lower-level interactions.

---

# 15. Ontological Rules

Rule 1

Players do not generate statistics directly.

Players generate decisions.

---

Rule 2

Decisions generate events.

---

Rule 3

Events generate statistics.

---

Rule 4

Statistics generate advanced metrics.

---

Rule 5

Advanced metrics never modify previous events.

---

Rule 6

Every entity exists before its variables.

---

Rule 7

Every variable exists before every formula.

---

Rule 8

Every formula exists before every prediction.

---

# 16. Ontological Integrity

No future specification may introduce concepts that violate this ontology.

New entities may be added.

Existing relationships may be extended.

Fundamental hierarchy may not be broken.

---

# 17. Final Statement

This ontology defines the complete conceptual universe of the NBA Universal Simulation Engine.

Every future document contained inside the NUSE specification SHALL assume this ontology as its conceptual foundation.
