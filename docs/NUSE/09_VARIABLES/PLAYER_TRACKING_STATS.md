# Player Tracking Statistics

## Purpose

This document defines all player tracking variables used by the NBA Universal Simulation Engine (NUSE).

Tracking variables describe how a player moves, interacts with teammates and opponents, controls the ball, creates space and generates offensive and defensive value.

Unlike traditional statistics, these variables are primarily derived from optical tracking systems.

Within NUSE they are considered first-class predictive variables.

---

# 1. Core Principles

Tracking variables measure behaviour.

They describe:

- Movement
- Positioning
- Ball interaction
- Spatial influence
- Defensive influence
- Offensive influence

Tracking variables often explain WHY traditional statistics occur.

# 20. Projection Importance

Tracking variables are among the strongest predictors of:

- Aging
- Skill sustainability
- Defensive development
- Offensive scalability
- System fit
- Injury risk
- Minutes stability
- Athletic decline

Projection models SHALL treat tracking variables as leading indicators.

---

# 21. General Rules

Tracking variables SHALL:

- Be possession-based whenever possible.
- Preserve temporal ordering.
- Be normalized when appropriate.
- Remain independent of implementation source.
- Support multiple tracking providers.

---

# Final Statement

Tracking variables provide the spatial and behavioural layer of the NBA Universal Simulation Engine.

They bridge the gap between observable statistics and the underlying actions that generate them, making them essential for high-fidelity player projection.
